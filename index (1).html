The moments that decide a member relationship

Choosing a plan, using it for the first time, getting help when something goes wrong, and deciding whether to stay shape the member relationship. I design the work around those moments, assign ownership, and measure the outcome. My tools are a journey map for the member's side and a service blueprint for the work behind it.

Does this plan fit this person's doctors, prescriptions, and life?

Every later moment is judged against what was promised here. I study which sales and enrollment behaviors go with members who stay, then build coaching and verification around those behaviors instead of around scripts.

What happens the first time they use the plan?

The first doctor visit and the first prescription are where a good enrollment gets confirmed or lost. I design the handoff from sale to service, with planned follow-up at 30, 60, and 90 days so problems surface while there is still time to fix them.

When a problem crosses teams, who gets back to the member?

Whether it's a stalled application, a billing error, or a complaint, I assign someone to follow up, set a deadline, and check that the issue was resolved. In 2025, our enrollment recovery work protected $11.3M in application value.

What do we do when the plan itself changes?

In 2025, my team reached 144,000 members whose coverage was at risk. More than 23,000 changed plans and stayed with eHealth.

Voice of the member, built from scratch

I established eHealth's NPS program and the reporting that turns member feedback into action. Findings go to the teams responsible for improvements, and we follow up with members.

Listen

Three sources in one view: the NPS program, public reviews, and what members post unprompted on social and review sites.

Respond

I designed a service recovery function, so a member who reports a problem gets one owner and a resolution. I also answer public reviews myself, under my own name.

Read

Every unhappy review and every detractor comment gets read and assigned one main reason. Scores say how members feel. Reasons say what to fix.

Report

The executive team gets a mid-season report while there is still time to act. It shows what changed against last year, the main reasons behind unhappy feedback, and what to watch before the season ends. Each finding goes to the team that owns the fix.

Mobilizing people, in practice

A member can be happy with their Medicare Advantage plan and still get a letter saying it will end. I built Operation Protect Our Book so our advocates could help those members choose coverage for the coming year.

1%
of Medicare Advantage enrollees were forced out of their plan in a typical year, 2018 through 2024
10%
were forced out heading into 2026, as insurers left markets
2.8M
people are in individual Medicare Advantage drug plans that end on December 31, 2026. Two years earlier it was 1.3 million

Industry figures: research published in JAMA, February 2026, and KFF analysis of CMS data, October 2026.

  1. The moment

    A termination notice turns a settled member into a shopper overnight. They did nothing wrong, and now they have a deadline. How they are treated in those weeks decides whether they stay.

  2. The rally

    I named the effort Operation Protect Our Book and used the kickoff to make the responsibility clear: help affected members understand their options and take action before their coverage ended.

  3. The work

    We built the muscle early: identify the members whose plans are ending, be ready when they call, and help each one into a plan that fits. I led the end-to-end design, which touched more than ten teams and passed through six phase gates.

  4. The result

    As the number of affected members grew, the share who cancelled and left fell year over year.
